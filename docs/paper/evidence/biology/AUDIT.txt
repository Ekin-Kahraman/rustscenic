RustScenic biological validation: independent return audit
6 November 2026

VERDICT
Execution succeeded. Biological evidence is mixed: useful lineage signals and
broadly similar RNA reference recovery, but a reproducible disadvantage in the
prespecified shared-TF classification comparison. Biological equivalence or
superiority is not established. The existing speed and memory results remain
valid for the documented configured workflows.

There is enough evidence to start and finish a carefully scoped software and
scalability manuscript that reports these trade-offs. This is not a green light
for a claim that RustScenic replaces SCENIC+ without loss of biological quality,
nor evidence of superior enhancer or causal predictions. No further atlas timing
run would resolve the present biological limitations. Journal suitability and
acceptance cannot be determined from execution completion alone.

2. The primary biological test

BMMC paired RNA/ATAC, GSE194122. Seven training donors, three unseen test donors;
10,000 test cells. Two training sizes, three algorithm seeds. Both methods use
the same 5,000 genes, 20,000 regions, 1,176 candidate TFs, AUCell scorer, classifier
and test cells. Only common TF names are used in the primary comparison; their
target-gene sets can differ. Means below give each donor equal weight, with
algorithm seeds averaged within donors.

Training cells   Rust macro-F1   SCENIC+ macro-F1   Difference
5,000            0.36885         0.42677           -0.05792
20,000           0.48198         0.51249           -0.03050

At 20,000 cells, balanced accuracy is 0.53465 versus 0.55691 (-0.02225).
The macro-F1 deficit remains in all three held-out donors after averaging seeds.
The reported donor-bootstrap interval is [-0.03328, -0.02700] at 20,000, but only
three test donors make that interval too limited to treat as precise population
evidence. No noninferiority margin was prespecified. Do not call this equivalent.

All returned TF signatures, still using the shared scorer:
Training cells   Rust macro-F1   SCENIC+ macro-F1   Rust/ref TF counts
5,000            0.62346         0.42677           208-218 / 6-12
20,000           0.63844         0.51363           152-157 / 14-22

This larger-feature-set advantage is useful descriptive evidence, not a fair
demonstration of better individual networks. The files call these results
"native", but here that means each method's full signature set, not native
activity scoring. Use "all signatures, common scorer" in the manuscript.

3. Does this explain the lower F1?

Partly. Several alternatives can now be ruled out or localised:

- Scoring alone: the deficit persists with identical AUCell scoring. Native and
  reference scoring are similar, not identical: median Spearman correlations
  at 20,000 cells are 0.927 on Rust signatures and 0.956 on reference signatures.
- TF self-expression: removing each TF's own gene changes reference shared-TF
  F1 from 0.51249 to 0.51139. Rust stays at 0.48198. The residual gap is 0.02940,
  so this explains very little of it.
- Target selection: median shared-TF gene-set Jaccard is 0.228 at 20,000 cells.
  Median common-signature sizes are 141 genes for Rust and 92 for SCENIC+.
  The remaining difference therefore lies in the learned signature composition
  and its interaction with the fixed classifier, not simply the scorer.
  These checks do not identify one uniquely responsible algorithmic step.
- Cell types: the largest common-TF F1 deficits at 20,000 are naive B cells
  (-0.198), CD8 T cells (-0.139) and ID2-high myeloid progenitors (-0.119).
  Rust is better for pDCs (+0.129), so the deficit is not uniform across biology.
  These are exploratory cell-type diagnostics, not prespecified significance tests.
- All-signature classification is partly nonspecific: for seed777 at 20,000,
  Rust F1 is 0.63166, versus 0.58747 for five size-matched random-target controls.
  The corresponding reference values are 0.49853 and 0.18819. Random gene sets
  can still encode cell identity when many features are provided. This result
  weakens use of classification alone as proof of correct regulatory edges.
- Label permutations are low (roughly 0.03-0.04 F1), supporting genuine label
  information in the unpermuted scores. Expression PCA remains stronger than
  either network representation: F1 0.76286 at 20,000 training cells.

The package narrows the explanation to network/signature construction and
coverage. It does not prove that changing any one threshold will safely fix it.
Do not choose thresholds on these test donors and then present the same donors
as an untouched confirmation set.

4. What biological evidence is positive, and what is weak?

Lineage plausibility:
- Rust recovers SPI1 and GATA1 in all six configurations. At 20,000 training
  cells their mean held-out lineage AUROCs are 0.9951 and 0.9959 respectively.
- PAX5, EBF1 and TCF7 are absent from both methods' final networks in all six.
  All are present in expression, candidate TFs, initial GRNs and motif-library
  annotations. Their motifs fail the selected region-set enrichment stage in
  both methods. This localises a coverage limitation of the configured workflow;
  it is not a gene-name mismatch or evidence that these regulators are absent
  from the biology. The exact reason within region/topic selection and enrichment
  requires further investigation if broad lineage recovery is a central claim.
- Reference SPI1 appears in two of three 20,000-cell seeds and GATA1 in one;
  both show strong expected lineage activity when present.

ATAC utility:
At 20,000 training cells, common-TF region-activity F1 is 0.19277 for Rust and
0.21355 for the reference. All-signature scores are 0.27478 and 0.21426.
These use observed ATAC counts with the common scorer. They do not validate
functional enhancer-to-gene links or reproduce native imputed-ATAC scoring.

RNA experimental references, mean average precision after motif refinement:
Reference          Rust       pySCENIC    Reference prevalence
mESC ChIP          0.29273    0.29287     0.29063
mESC perturbation  0.11712    0.11784     0.10958
hESC ChIP          0.14803    0.14757     0.14728
mDC ChIP           0.04795    0.04764     0.04607

These are descriptively close between methods, but close to the prevalence
baseline in several settings. The original absolute-Pearson baseline beats
both original inference methods on all four reference comparisons. Motif
pruning does not reverse that overall AP comparison.

Pruning retains a small, partly enriched subset: Rust's retained-edge support
is 1.13-2.30 times a degree-matched uniform baseline, while recall of known
reference links is only 0.57-1.59%. Against the original GRN at the same per-TF
edge budget, enrichment improves in mESC/hESC but not mDC (0.80 times baseline).
Report both support and coverage; a high fraction among a tiny retained set
does not demonstrate strong whole-network recovery.

Experimental truth covers 81/620 TFs for mESC ChIP, 34/620 for its perturbations,
34/410 for hESC and 18/321 for mDC. Unlisted edges are unlabelled, not confirmed
negatives. ChIP binding and perturbation response are not proof of direct causal
regulation. These references support a limited retrieval comparison, not a
global probability that a predicted edge is biologically correct.

Seed stability is also mixed. At 20,000 cells Rust recovers TF identities more
consistently (mean TF Jaccard 0.809 versus 0.481), while common-TF target sets are
less stable (mean of pairwise median target Jaccards 0.344 versus 0.558).
Whole TF-gene edge Jaccard is similar (0.425 versus 0.422). No universal stability
advantage follows from these different measures.

5. Audit limitations and reporting requirements

- Three unseen donors from one BMMC cohort are not three independent cohorts.
  Seeds are algorithm repeats. The 5k experiment uses the vocabulary selected
  on the 20k training pool, but never the test donors; it is not a completely
  independent 5k feature-selection experiment.
- Macro-F1 is averaged over cell types actually present in each test donor.
  These donors contain 16-21 cell types, with the smallest present class having
  six cells. Predictions of classes absent from a donor produce the observed
  sklearn warnings, not failed runs. Recomputing macro-F1 over the union of
  true and predicted classes lowers scores but preserves the main direction.
- The reused HDMA analysis evaluates 22 donors; HDMA-4 has insufficient sampled
  cells for its fold. Some HDMA test labels do not occur in classifier training
  donors and remain errors. Its network fitting is transductive, unlike the new
  BMMC experiment. Do not conflate these two validation designs.
- RNA motif databases are species-correct despite the historical mouse-gene
  filename alias. pySCENIC reports modules skipped below 80% gene mapping:
  521 mESC, 84 hESC and 1 mDC warnings across the three seeds. Native pruning has
  different filtering rules. Include mapping coverage and algorithm differences.
- Both pipelines share a deliberately restricted gene/region panel and fixed
  topic settings. SCENIC+ biological claims are broader than this configured
  benchmark. These are configured workflow comparisons, not proof of a purely
  language-level speedup or a lossless, default-for-default replacement.
- No independent functional enhancer-to-gene validation was performed. TF-union
  signatures also combine contexts. This limits mechanistic claims.
- Small cross-runtime classifier differences were retained, not silently replaced
  with favourable recomputed values. Raw outputs and scripts are unchanged.

6. What to do before submission

Proceed with the software/scalability manuscript and use these results to state
the biological trade-offs explicitly. Finish the methods, figures, code/data
archive and a complete parameter/feature-coverage comparison. Include weak and
negative controls, not just the favourable all-signature and lineage plots.

If the intended paper requires "preserved biological accuracy" or broad
SCENIC+ replacement, the current evidence does not meet that claim. The next
work would be a targeted diagnostic/repair of signature construction and motif
coverage, followed by validation on independent data. More cells or another
speed run cannot substitute for that. Do not automatically repeat the atlas jobs.
